Improve Pasture Mapping in the Cerrado Biome

Pastures represent the largest anthropogenic land use in the Cerrado biome, yet conventional remote sensing products typically map pasture as a single homogeneous land-use class at 30 m spatial resolution. In practice, Cerrado pastures span a broad ecological and management gradient — ranging from well-managed, high-biomass forage systems (Productive Pasture) to areas affected by ruderal weed infestation (Pasture with Weeds), woody encroachment (Pasture with Shrubs), intermediate vigor loss (Intermediate Pasture), severe Biological Degradation with exposed soil, or Natural Regeneration returning to native savanna physiognomies. Differentiating these seven pasture condition categories at 10-meter spatial resolution is essential to identify priority areas for restoration and sustainable intensification, reduce conversion pressure on remnant native vegetation, improve carbon and greenhouse-gas accounting, and guide territorial investments with quantifiable uncertainty.
