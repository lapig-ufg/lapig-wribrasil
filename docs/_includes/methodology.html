Product 1 · Detailed Sampling Plan · Delivered 17/08/2026

The database with the highest quality control: 701 sample areas surveyed in the field by Embrapa Cenargen, each a polygon of approximately 1 ha. Of these, 608 have a defined class according to the seven categories established by the Embrapa team; the 93 units without a defined class are retained with their own identification.

  • 608 classified samples: the references for label transfer and for characterizing the SOM neurons.
  • 93 unlabeled samples: may participate in unsupervised steps (e.g., SOM training), but cannot define neuron categories, contribute to Top-k voting, or be counted as classified references.
Class dictionary harmonization. Preliminary materials record a five-class proposal and a later seven-category implementation. The final database must contain a single, versioned dictionary approved by the Embrapa–LAPIG team (code, name, operational definition, quantitative criteria, year of observation, responsible party, change history).

Dual metric extraction for every point

For each area and spectral index, a monthly series was built in Google Earth Engine. Each month uses a centered window of ~60 days (second half of the previous month, the full month, and the first half of the next). The value is the joint median of all valid pixel-date pairs after excluding clouds and shadows:

Ĩ(i,k,m) = median { I(i,k,p,t) : p ∈ polygon i; t ∈ window m; valid observation }
  • References: ~100 pixels per 1 ha polygon at 10 m (depends on geometry and grid alignment).
  • Candidates: 3 × 3 pixel window (900 m² / 0.09 ha) — joint median of the nine pixels; may cross edges between pasture and other covers.
  • Indices: NDVI, EVI, NDII, NDWI, PSRI, and CAI — 12 monthly values each per record; bands at native 20 m require an explicit statement of resampling to 10 m.

Annual AlphaEarth Foundations Satellite Embedding vectors (GOOGLE/SATELLITE_EMBEDDING/V1/ANNUAL; Brown et al., 2025) summarize a calendar year of multi-sensor observation for each 10 m pixel. For each polygon, the component-wise mean of the included pixels gives one vector representative of the ~1 ha support (for candidates, the mean of the nine 3 × 3 vectors):

ē(i,a,d) = (1 / n(i,a)) × Σ e(p,a,d),   d = 0,…,63
Normalization. Original embeddings have unit norm, but the mean of unit vectors does not. The dot product equals cosine similarity only for unit vectors, so comparison uses explicit cosine or the dot product after L2 renormalization:
cos(x,y) = (x · y) / (‖x‖₂ × ‖y‖₂)

The field label remains anchored to its year of observation; other years do not inherit the label without verifying temporal stability.

Product 2 · Field and Image-Based Sample Collection · Delivered 03/11/2026

Each candidate record is compared with the 608 classified Embrapa references in the 64-dimensional space using cosine similarity. The most similar reference defines the preliminary Top-1 category, and md3 counts how many of the three nearest references share it. Top-1 similarity (prod_escalar_1) and md3 were computed for all 428,866 records; the transferred category feeds the SOM stage.

3High agreementAll three nearest references share the same category.
2Moderate agreementTwo of the three nearest references agree.
1Low agreementOnly the Top-1 assigns the class — ambiguous profile.

md3 = 3 represents local agreement, not accuracy or calibrated probability.

Transferred category + Sentinel-2 series

The Sentinel-2 series of the candidate records are organized in a SOM (Kohonen, 1990), which preserves neighborhood relationships so that points with similar phenology cluster together regardless of spatial distance. Each neuron and its neighborhood are characterized by the dominant category, its purity, and a posterior probability, and each record receives one of three decisions:

Category differs from the neighborhood's dominant category?Yes → Remove
Category matches and posterior supports it?Yes → Clean (keep for training)
Category matches, posterior support insufficient→ Analyze: route to CBERS-4A inspection

Configurations tested

  • 35 × 35 grid (1,225 neurons) and a single 90 × 90 grid (8,100 neurons) trained jointly on all years.
  • Normal-Normal and Dirichlet-Multinomial smoothing; the two models agree on 98.4% of records, and the two grids on 73.0% (disagreement concentrated outside productive pasture).
  • Final configuration: 90 × 90 global grid with Dirichlet-Multinomial smoothing — one model for all years keeps neuron labels consistent across the series.

Stratified subsample (Clean + Analyze, 2020–2025)

Only 2020–2025 records are eligible (CBERS-4A imagery is available from 2020 onward) and Remove records are excluded, giving an eligible universe of 245,012 records (61,125 locations).

  • Allocation: equal per category instead of proportional, so that rare categories are measured — 100 locations per Clean stratum and 50 per Analyze stratum; all Clean locations of Pasto com Lenhosas (62) and Miscelânea (59) were taken (census).
  • One year per location, randomly drawn, with a fixed seed (20261103); each point carries its stratum size and design weight.
  • Precision: about ±10 p.p. per Clean stratum (100 points) and ±14 p.p. per Analyze stratum (50 points), 95% confidence, worst case.
  • Protocol: corroborated / not corroborated / inconclusive, observed category, image date, and justification; preliminary category hidden whenever possible; a fraction interpreted by two analysts.
Interpretation not yet started. The results will measure the quality of the training labels by category, show whether Analyze records should be recovered, and indicate whether rare categories should be merged (component 12).
In parallel · pasture base map

A new pasture / non-pasture training set built only from visually interpreted samples: no sample was drawn from existing maps and no map-based filter was applied, addressing the circularity risk raised in Product 1.

  • The same 36,520 locations each year, with the label of that year: about 12,500 pasture and 24,000 non-pasture samples (about 2 non-pasture per pasture).
  • Each point linked to the IBGE map sheet where the map is generated; maximum 500 samples per sheet.
  • Five sheets on the biome border have no samples and will need neighbor-sheet models or added samples.

Sentinel-2 vigor maps for 2025 were generated for three candidate pasture base maps:

  • 2025a: Random Forest with the MapBiomas Sentinel-2 methodology and the new visually interpreted training set.
  • 2025b: same as 2025a, with geographic coordinates as additional variables.
  • Persistent: MapBiomas Sentinel-2 pasture stable from 2020 to 2025.

Field check of degradation × 2025 vigor

The 2022 Goiás survey holds 1,901 interpreter evaluations of 562 points. A degradation score (mean rating, 1–7) was grouped into three classes (ND, PD, SD); 413 points have both a score and a 2025 vigor class. The association is weak (Spearman ρ = −0.14): 33% of points agree, 53% disagree by one class, and 15% disagree totally.

  • Selection: 11 or 12 points drawn at random in each of the nine degradation × vigor strata (three agreement, four partial-disagreement, two total-disagreement), 100 in total, with design weights referring to the 413 points.
  • Purpose: show whether each disagreement comes from change since 2022 (management, reform, conversion) or from error in the vigor map; agreement strata act as controls.
  • Routes: two campaigns from the UFG Samambaia campus — south/southwest/west Goiás (46 points, 1,894 km) and north/northeast Goiás (54 points, 2,163 km), six days each.
  • Protocol: the 2022 protocol (forage species, height, ground cover, invasive plants, termite mounds, forage availability, condition, degradation, management, photographs); incorporating the Embrapa field protocol is under evaluation.
Scope of inference. The revisit covers the 413 surveyed points in Goiás; it is not an accuracy estimate for the Cerrado.
Product 3 · Annual 10 m Pasture Maps & Field Points · Due 01/12/2026

A stratified random sample of 940 points over the eight strata of the 2025a × 2025b × MapBiomas crossing, with equal probability within each stratum; the difference strata receive many more points than their area. A single sample estimates the accuracy of 2025a, 2025b, MapBiomas, and Global Pasture Watch and answers two questions with reference data: is 2025a better than 2025b, and does the new map improve on MapBiomas?

2025a has the advantage of not using coordinates as predictors.

CBERS-4A corroboration rates
  • The corroboration rate of Clean records, by category, measures the quality of the training labels.
  • The corroboration rate of Analyze records shows whether that group should be recovered for training or discarded.
  • Low corroboration in the rare categories (Pasto com Lenhosas, Miscelânea) would support merging them; any grouping depends on ecological coherence, spectral-temporal separability, and performance under independent validation.
Chosen base map + approved samples
  • Random Forest in Google Earth Engine, trained with the 36,520 visually interpreted points per year.
  • The five border sheets without samples require neighbor-sheet models or added samples.
  • Vigor classes mapped on the chosen pasture base for each year.
  • Only samples meeting the quality criteria of the embedding, SOM, and inspection analyses are eligible; rejected or inconclusive samples stay archived for auditing.
  • The final set is evaluated for category balance, spatial distribution, coverage of vigor strata, and redundancy.
  • Training and evaluation are split by spatial blocks to minimize autocorrelation leakage (Breiman, 2001).
Product 4 · Property-Level Trajectories, DCF Indicators & Pasture Restoration · Due 05/01/2027

Rural property boundaries (SIGEF, SICAR) and permanent preservation areas (FBDS) are assembled as the spatial units for the property-level analyses of components 16–18.

Cross-tabulation of the pasture maps with MapBiomas (1985–2025) and PRODES to reconstruct land-use trajectories, with environmental liabilities and assets aggregated by rural property, municipality, state, biome, and watershed.

  • Soy conversion potential from land use, native vegetation cover, pasture degradation, slope/aptitude, and property boundaries (2024–2030 scenarios).
  • Forest Code compliance balance: Legal Reserve and APP deficits and surpluses per property.

Pasture restoration potential per rural property, combining GPP trends, vegetative vigor, bare soil exposure, and historical land-use persistence derived from the 2020–2025 maps and the trajectories of component 16.

Cross-cutting · applies to every stage

Each sample keeps a stable identifier and fields for provenance, geometry, year, source, label, confidence level, similarity metrics, SOM neuron, inspection outcome, and change history. Scripts, parameters, and collection versions are preserved for reproducibility.
