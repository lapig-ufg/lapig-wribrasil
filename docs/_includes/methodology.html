The database with the highest quality control: 701 sample areas surveyed in the field by Embrapa Cenargen, each a polygon of approximately 1 ha. Of these, 608 have a defined class according to the seven categories established by the Embrapa team; the 93 units without a defined class are retained with their own identification.

  • 608 classified samples: the references for label transfer and coherence analysis.
  • 93 unlabeled samples: may participate in unsupervised steps (e.g., SOM training), but cannot define neuron categories, contribute to Top-k voting, or be counted as classified references.
Class dictionary harmonization. Preliminary materials record a five-class proposal and a later seven-category implementation. The final database must contain a single, versioned dictionary approved by the Embrapa–LAPIG team (code, name, operational definition, quantitative criteria, year of observation, responsible party, change history). Integrity rule: no category distribution or label transfer is presented as definitive before this reconciliation.

Agreement with MapBiomas Collection 11 (Table 3)

Collection 11 corroborated pasture at 44,943 of the 50,000 points (89.886%) and disagreed at 5,057 (10.114%). Disagreement does not prove a point is not pasture — neither map is ground truth — so both scenarios are kept for sensitivity analysis:

  • Inclusive: 62,009 candidates — keeps disagreeing cases to test robustness and rejection.
  • Agreement: 56,952 candidates — restricted to points corroborated by both collections.
Dual metric extraction for every point

For each area and spectral index, a monthly series was built from January 2020 to December 2025 in Google Earth Engine. Each month uses a centered window of ~60 days (second half of the previous month, the full month, and the first half of the next). The value is the joint median of all valid pixel-date pairs after excluding clouds and shadows:

Ĩ(i,k,m) = median { I(i,k,p,t) : p ∈ polygon i; t ∈ window m; valid observation }
  • References: ~100 pixels per 1 ha polygon at 10 m (depends on geometry and grid alignment).
  • Candidates: 3 × 3 pixel window (900 m² / 0.09 ha) — joint median of the nine pixels; may cross edges between pasture and other covers.
  • Indices: vigor, moisture, senescence, and soil exposure; bands at native 20 m require an explicit statement of resampling to 10 m.
  • Metadata: pixel-date pairs, dates represented, valid spatial proportion, and gaps per composite.
Pending control: rule for January 2020 and December 2025 (edge months), minimum number of observations, and cloud/shadow masking audit.

Annual AlphaEarth Foundations Satellite Embedding vectors (GOOGLE/SATELLITE_EMBEDDING/V1/ANNUAL; Brown et al., 2025) summarize a calendar year of multi-sensor observation for each 10 m pixel. For each polygon, the component-wise mean of the included pixels gives one vector representative of the ~1 ha support (for candidates, the mean of the nine 3 × 3 vectors):

ē(i,a,d) = (1 / n(i,a)) × Σ e(p,a,d),   d = 0,…,63

Pixels partially intersected by the polygon are included in full, without area weighting, which can increase mixing in narrow or transitional areas.

Normalization. Original embeddings have unit norm, but the mean of unit vectors does not. The dot product equals cosine similarity only for unit vectors, so L2 norms are computed before the final filtering and comparison uses explicit cosine or the dot product after renormalization:
cos(x,y) = (x · y) / (‖x‖₂ × ‖y‖₂)

The field label remains anchored to its year of observation; other years do not inherit the label without verifying temporal stability.

Two independent representation spaces

Each candidate is compared with the Embrapa references in the 64-dimensional space using cosine similarity (after norm correction). The most similar reference defines the preliminary Top-1 label; the three highest values form the Top-3, and md3 counts how many of the three share the Top-1 category.

3High agreementAll three nearest references share the same category.
2Moderate agreementTwo of the three nearest references agree.
1Low agreementOnly the Top-1 assigns the class — ambiguous profile.

md3 = 3 represents local agreement, not accuracy or calibrated probability.

Correct interpretation. This stage selects the most consistent pseudo-labels in the embedding space ("the broad net"). It does not replace independent validation, and it cannot detect land-use changes between 2020 and 2025 — which motivates the temporal filter in stage 2. Retention is reported by category, origin, vigor, region, and scenario.
Approved candidates + 701 references

Candidates approved in stage 1 are combined with the 701 Embrapa areas and their Sentinel-2 series are organized in a SOM (Kohonen, 1990), which preserves neighborhood relationships so that points with similar phenology cluster together regardless of spatial distance. Each sample is assigned to its best-matching neuron, and the class from the embeddings is checked against the composition of the neuron and its neighborhood.

The check: a candidate labeled "Productive Pasture" by the embeddings that lands in a neuron dominated by degraded field samples reveals a temporal anomaly. Because candidates far outnumber references, the SOM is a spectral-temporal consistency filter, not independent validation.

Dimensionality and preparation

  • One index over one annual cycle = 12 values; with J indices = 12 × J; six complete years = 72 × J. Alternatives compared: one SOM per index, a multivariate annual SOM, or an inter-annual monthly climatology.
  • Standardize each variable; define treatment of missing values and low-availability months.
  • Test grid dimensions, seeds, and initializations; record quantization and topographic errors, neuron occupancy, and stability.
  • The 93 unlabeled references do not contribute to neuron labeling.

Cleaning rule — decision matrix

Dirichlet-Multinomial smoothing of category counts (prioritized; Normal-Normal kept for comparison) combines each neuron's local composition with its hexagonal neighborhood:

Prior P(k | j) < τc?Yes → Remove (noisy / outlier)
Posterior P(k | xj) ≥ τp?Yes → Keep for training
Otherwise (ambiguous)→ Analyze: route to CBERS-4A inspection

Thresholds, the α₀ pseudo-count, neighborhood size, and minimum references per neuron will be defined by sensitivity analysis.

Self-reference risk. If class frequencies come mostly from the candidates' own pseudo-labels, the SOM may simply reproduce the embedding assignment. Evidence from Embrapa references and internal candidate coherence are reported separately.
Stratified subsample (approved + ambiguous)

Applied to a subsample of points approved in the embedding–SOM stages, to estimate the overall quality of the pre-selected set, identify systematic errors, and guide adjustments before the final Random Forest training.

Not map validation. Map validation occurs after the cartographic product is generated. Image interpretation does not replace field observation for all pasture condition categories.

Initial sample sizing

Simple random sampling with finite population correction, for an illustrative N = 40,000, margin of error 3%, 95% confidence, and p = 0.5:

n = N·z²·p(1−p) / [ e²(N−1) + z²·p(1−p) ] ≈ 1,040 points

This estimates a single global binary proportion (label corroborated or not), not a 3% margin per category. The final sample will be stratified by category, confidence scenario, and, when needed, region and vigor, with minimum representation for rare classes and weights for global estimates.

Interpretation protocol

  • Image date compatible with the sample year.
  • Pseudo-label hidden from the interpreter whenever possible (reduces confirmation bias).
  • Decision recorded as corroborated, not corroborated, or inconclusive, with justification and image quality.
  • A fraction submitted to two independent interpreters; agreement measured and disagreements adjudicated.
  • Link preserved between image, date, interpreter, protocol version, and final decision.
Approved training set

Only candidates meeting the quality criteria of the embedding, SOM, and inspection analyses are eligible. Labels retain evidence level, origin, and decision history; rejected or inconclusive samples stay archived for auditing but are not used automatically as positive cases.

  • The final set is evaluated for category balance, spatial distribution, coverage of vigor strata, and redundancy.
  • Rare categories are not merged merely to increase counts; any grouping depends on ecological coherence, spectral-temporal separability, and performance under independent validation.
  • The Random Forest produces the first 10 m Cerrado pasture map differentiating the viable categories; training and evaluation are split by spatial blocks to minimize autocorrelation leakage.
Map v1 (10 m)

After the first version of the map is generated, image-based and field-based evaluations follow specific statistical designs. One hundred points suit a pilot campaign and the detection of systematic errors, but do not ensure high precision for seven categories across the biome (≈ ±8 percentage points for an accuracy near 80% under simple random sampling, before design effects).

  • Keep independence between training and validation points.
  • Seek spatially balanced allocation across categories, confidence levels, ecoregions, and environmental gradients.
  • Record inclusion probabilities and weight when allocation is disproportionate.
  • Control clustering along routes and enforce a minimum distance between localities.
  • Treat the concentration in Goiás as a regional priority validation, not automatic inference for the whole Cerrado.

Sampling plan, estimators, confidence intervals, and uncertainty analysis will be defined with Prof. Luis Rodrigo Fernandes Baumann (IME/UFG) and the LAPIG field and remote sensing team.

Cross-cutting · applies to every stage

Each sample keeps a stable identifier and fields for provenance, geometry, year, source, label, confidence level, similarity metrics, SOM neuron, inspection outcome, and change history. Scripts, parameters, and collection versions are preserved for reproducibility.
